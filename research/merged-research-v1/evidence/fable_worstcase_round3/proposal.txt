# Round 3 synthesis: integer-lattice lift, its adversary, and the missing lemma

**Verdict.** I have one concrete strengthening that provably removes the Fano 1-in-3 bottleneck, but no defensible route to the all-input theorem. The strengthening is defeated by a scalable family below, and the first unproved step is the same one as before: bounding total branch states. Everything here is hand-derived; nothing was run or formalized.

## 1. Route: lift the affine shortcut from GF(2) to ℤ (E-MRA-Z)

**State.** Each state holds the canonical clause set, the witness history, and an integer system `Ax = b` in Hermite normal form.

**Discovery (syntactic, polynomial).**
- An XOR block on a set S becomes `Σ_S x = c + 2s`, with s a fresh integer slack.
- A positive clause on S plus all pairwise binaries `(¬x_i ∨ ¬x_j)` becomes `Σ_S x = 1`, with no slack.

**Refutation rule (integer Farkas, sound direction only).** If a rational y has `yᵀA` integral and `yᵀb` non-integral, the state is UNSAT.

**Checkable lemma L1 (Lean-sized).** If `yᵀA ∈ ℤⁿ` and `yᵀb ∉ ℤ`, then `Ax = b` has no integer solution, hence no 0/1 solution.

**What it buys, checked by hand:**
- **K5 odd-charge Tseitin:** y = ½·**1** gives `Σ_e x_e − Σ_v s_v = (Σc)/2`, which is non-integral. This reproduces your affine result.
- **Fano 1-in-3:** y = ⅓·**1** gives `Σx = 7/3`, since each point lies on 3 lines. This is a root refutation of the instance GF(2) leaves at rank 4 with 8 parity solutions.

**Safety constraints.**
- Lattice feasibility proves nothing, because it ignores the 0/1 bounds. It never produces SAT.
- Only two-variable unimodular equations (`x = y`, `x = 1 − y`) may be substituted into clauses.
- Missing-variable defaults, replay, never rebranching eliminated variables, and unit retention are unchanged.
- Hermite normal form is polynomial in bit length (Kannan–Bachem), so no coefficient blow-up is hidden.

## 2. The exact theorem that would be needed (conjecture T, unproved)

There is a polynomial p such that for every CNF F of encoded length L, the run of E-MRA-Z satisfies:

- **(a) Total states:** the number of states created, root plus all branch states, is at most p(L).
- **(b) Representation:** each state's clauses, Hermite basis, and history occupy at most p(L) bits.
- **(c) Discovery and bit work:** the work per state is at most p(L) bit operations.
- **(d) Correctness:** the output is correct, with SAT answers replay-verified.

Status of each part:
- **(b)** looks provable. The count guard keeps the clause count at most m, widths are at most n, and Hermite entries are polynomial.
- **(c)** is routine.
- **(d)** reduces to your compiled DP identities plus L1.
- **(a)** is the whole problem, and it implies P = NP. Nothing in your evidence bears on it: 19,242 passing runs and 1,500 random formulas test correctness and typical behaviour, not worst-case growth.

## 3. Self-attack: NAE on Steiner triple systems

**Family A_v** (v ≡ 1, 3 mod 6, v ≥ 7). Variables are the points of a Steiner triple system. Each triple {a,b,c} gives the clauses `(a∨b∨c)` and `(¬a∨¬b∨¬c)`. There are v(v−1)/3 clauses.

**UNSAT for all v ≥ 7.** Write y = ±1. Not-all-equal on a triple is equivalent to `y_a y_b + y_b y_c + y_a y_c = −1`. Each pair lies in exactly one triple, so summing gives `Σ_{i<j} y_i y_j = −v(v−1)/6`. Hence `(Σy)² = v − v(v−1)/3`, which is negative for v > 4.

**Why every current shortcut is silent:**
- **XOR extraction:** each triple supplies one clause of the odd XOR and one of the even XOR, so there are 0 parity equations.
- **Cardinality detection:** there are no binary clauses, so there are 0 slack-free equations.
- **Lattice with slack:** `x_a + x_b + x_c − s = 1` is always integer-feasible.
- **Linear relaxation:** x = ½ is feasible.

The certificate that works is degree 2. The lattice lift is one rung of a ladder, not growth control.

**Test (7 variables, 14 clauses).** Lines: 123, 145, 167, 246, 257, 347, 356. Order: index. Predictions:

1. Root shortcuts find 0 XOR equations and 0 cardinality equations, and no refutation.
2. Every variable has pos·neg = 3·3 = 9, so the tie goes to x1.
3. Eliminating x1 gives 9 resolvent pairs. Three are tautologies (same line) and 6 are width-4 clauses, none subsumed. The count goes 14→14, so the guard accepts. The new clauses are:
   - `(2∨3∨¬4∨¬5)`, `(2∨3∨¬6∨¬7)`
   - `(4∨5∨¬2∨¬3)`, `(4∨5∨¬6∨¬7)`
   - `(6∨7∨¬2∨¬3)`, `(6∨7∨¬4∨¬5)`
4. Now every remaining variable has pos·neg = 4·4 = 16. For x2 there are 16 pairs, 6 tautologies, and 10 distinct unsubsumed resolvents, so the count would go 14→16. By the point-stabilizer symmetry the same holds for x3 to x7. The guard rejects all six and the solver must branch.
5. The final answer is UNSAT. I do not predict the branch count.

If step 3 or 4 disagrees with the implementation, then either my resolvent count or your canonicalization is wrong. Either outcome is informative.

**Scaling measurement that matters.** Run A_v for v = 7, 9, 13, 15, 19, 21 and record total states. I have no proved resolution lower bound for A_v, so this is an open measurement, not a claimed counterexample.

## 4. Why the repair does not close the gap

Adding a degree-2 rule (pair moments plus the square identity) kills A_v. The same counting argument rules out a 2-colouring of any 2-(v,k,1) design once v > k² − k + 1, so NAE on those designs is not a harder family. The escalation has to come from elsewhere.

Each repair raises the certificate degree by one. For the separate systems the ladder is known to be unbounded:
- Polynomial calculus over every field needs linear degree on random 3-CNF (Ben-Sasson–Impagliazzo; Alekhnovich–Razborov).
- Sum-of-squares needs linear degree on random 3-XOR and 3-SAT (Grigoriev; Schoenebeck).

I do not assert that these bounds transfer to a combined lattice-plus-moment closure. I believe they do, but that is a statement about one model, not about P versus NP.

A fixed-degree closure is also the bounded-width assumption you asked me not to recycle. I will not propose it as the route.

## 5. First unproved step, and the decisive missing idea

Part (a) of T needs a **progress lemma**:

> There is a polynomially bounded potential Φ on states and a polynomial-time branch rule such that Φ does not increase under UP, guarded DP, and lattice steps, and Φ(F) ≥ Φ(F|x=0) + Φ(F|x=1) at every branch.

Total states would then be at most Φ(root). No candidate survives:

- **Clause count, variable count, lattice rank:** none is superadditive. Both children can keep nearly everything.
- **Minimal refutation-tree size:** this is superadditive, but exponential on pigeonhole (Haken), so it is not polynomially bounded.
- **State merging to escape trees:** your per-state immutable histories make every state distinct. Merging would need histories shared as a DAG and a canonical quotient with polynomially many reachable classes. Clausal equivalence gives resolution DAGs, which Haken and Urquhart defeat for that quotient only.

**The decisive missing idea** is a polynomial-time canonical state quotient under which reachable classes are polynomially many on all inputs. Every quotient I can define is a bounded level of a known proof-system hierarchy, and each has unconditional lower bounds. Those bounds are barriers for those quotients, not evidence about quotients that use extension variables, where nothing is known either way.

One tempting fix I checked and discard: refactoring a rejected elimination's p×q resolvent grid with a fresh variable (bounded variable addition) reproduces the eliminated variable under a new name. It is a no-op that can cycle.

## 6. Recommended next actions

1. Formalize L1 and the two certificates (K5 with ½, Fano with ⅓). This is cheap and extends the Lean base.
2. Run the 7-variable test against predictions 1 to 4.
3. Measure the A_v state counts. Polynomial growth would be a surprise worth explaining, and exponential growth gives a clean benchmark that defeats both the affine and the lattice shortcut.
4. Treat any further tractable-family repair as benchmark engineering unless it comes with a candidate Φ.

`★ Insight ─────────────────────────────────────`
- The GF(2) shortcut is the "mod 2 shadow" of an integer lattice test. Fano 1-in-3 survives mod 2 but fails mod 3, which is why one Hermite computation subsumes parity reasoning for every modulus.
- NAE-on-Steiner needs a product of two variables to refute. This is the general pattern: each certificate degree has a natural family that is invisible one degree lower.
- Because the count guard already bounds representation size, the open problem is entirely about how many states exist, not how large each one is.
