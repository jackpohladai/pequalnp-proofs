Executable matching-certificate validation with actual CNF conclusions

All four Lean sources compiled successfully using pinned Lean 4.36.0.
Printed theorem axioms are limited to propext, Classical.choice, and
Quot.sound. Final source bytes, generated artifacts and imported component
sources/artifacts were checked against their successful compile receipts.
No generated candidate was executed. Live services, original component
source files and remote state were not changed.

Success certificate

A success certificate maps each Fin r row to Option (Fin h). Every active
row (guard false) must have some neighboring hole, and active rows cannot
share a hole. Inactive entries are ignored. This permits zero holes when
all rows are inactive, without requiring an impossible total row-to-hole
function. successCheck executes finite row and pair checks; successCheck_iff
proves the exact conditions, and accepted_success_has_matching proves soundness.

Failure certificate

A failure certificate is a finite set S of rows. failureCheck verifies that
every selected row is active and that the union of their neighbor sets is
strictly smaller than S. failureCheck_iff proves the exact conditions.
accepted_failure_no_matching derives impossibility of matching from the
checked Hall theorem. The empty set and sets containing inactive rows do
not incorrectly certify failure.

Semantic completeness, not a search algorithm

matching_iff_success_certificate and no_matching_iff_failure_certificate
prove that appropriate certificates exist for matching and nonmatching
instances. accepted_certificates_cannot_conflict proves that accepted yes
and no certificates cannot coexist. These theorems do not implement a
matching search or promise its runtime. Certificate production remains a
separate algorithmic obligation.

Actual CNF consequences

CertifiedCNFConclusions composes the validators with the preceding actual
CNF constructor and retained solver. Given checkedSolve F = some (some T),
positive guard clauses, an injective edge map and retained/guard separation,
an accepted success certificate at the guards of state (support F) T proves
the constructed CNF satisfiable. An accepted failure certificate at those
same guards proves it unsatisfiable.

The greatest-model and positive-guard premises must not be omitted. For
empty retained F, one guard NOT x and no holes, greatest x=true has an active
deficient row, but the complete CNF NOT x is satisfiable at x=false. This
counterexample is checked independently and included in generator feedback.

Concrete executable edge adapter

CertificateEdges defines certificateEdges g cert i k by testing whether
row i is active and cert i is some k. accepted_certificate_edges proves its
three exact private-edge conditions: only neighbors are true, every active
row has a true edge, and no hole has true edges from distinct rows.
accepted_certificate_private_edges packages this concrete witness.

Masking inactive rows is essential: an accepted certificate may store the
same hole in an active and an inactive row. Converting every stored Some
to a true edge would then violate column exclusivity. The proved adapter
sets all inactive edges false. Its source and accepted artifact were sent
to the separate executable witness-lift worker for integration.

Validation

MatchingCertificates compiled in 18.945 seconds; CertifiedCNFConclusions
in 8.001; CertificateExamples in 4.337; CertificateEdges in 16.245.
All exited zero. Actual Lean reductions cover valid matching, collisions,
missing active entries, non-neighbors, all-inactive/zero-hole inputs,
deficient cuts, empty or inactive cuts, and masking ignored entries.

Independent enumeration checked 15,025 success certificates and 5,039
failure certificates across 685 graph/guard instances. There were 4,155
accepted successes and 590 accepted failures. Each was checked against
independent exhaustive matching, and every accepted success's masked edge
witness was checked. Certificate existence and mutual exclusion held on
the entire finite corpus. These checks supplement the general Lean proofs.

Remaining boundaries

The interfaces are typed: Fin bounds row/hole IDs and Finset removes duplicate
rows. An external serialized-certificate parser is not verified here. It
must validate bounds, preserve the intended Option entries, and deduplicate
set certificates rather than count repeated rows as distinct.

Complete matching search, arbitrary-input CNF decomposition/recognition,
generated-code refinement and total encoded-bit runtime remain unproved.
This is not a general SAT solver or P=NP proof. Results are local Lean
checks, with no official checker replay, upstream acceptance or credit claim.
